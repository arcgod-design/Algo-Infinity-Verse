Routing Philosophies Explained

⭕

Chord: The Ring and the Fingers

Nodes and keys are mapped onto a circular identifier space (a consistent hash ring). Instead of keeping track of every node, a node maintains a Finger Table containing up to m entries (where 2^m is the ring size). The i-th finger points to the first node that succeeds the ID by at least 2^(i-1). This creates exponentially growing jump distances, allowing O(log n) greedy leaps toward any key.

📶

Skip Graph: Vector-Guided Levels

Skip Graphs distribute the idea of Skip Lists over a network. Every node receives a random binary "membership vector". Nodes are organized into layered linked lists based on how much of their prefix matches. Routing starts at the highest shared prefix level and drops down whenever it overshoots the target. Unlike Chord, it natively supports range queries and doesn't rely on consistent hashing.
