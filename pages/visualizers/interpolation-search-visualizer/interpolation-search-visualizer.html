The Mechanics of Prediction

📈

Linear Interpolation (The Math)

Instead of blind halving, it assumes the data grows steadily in a straight line. By finding the slope between the current `low` and `high` values, it mathematically projects exactly where the `target` value should intersect that line, directly jumping to that calculated index.

⚡

The O(log log n) Best Case

If the data is truly uniformly distributed (like a perfectly spaced sequence 10, 20, 30...), the formula becomes a hyper-accurate oracle. The algorithm jumps almost directly to the exact target instantly, yielding an average performance of O(log log n), far faster than Binary Search.

⚠️

The O(n) Skewed Trap

When data is skewed (e.g., exponentially growing), the straight-line assumption fails catastrophically. The formula chronically underestimates or overestimates the position, forcing the algorithm to creep forward index-by-index in tiny increments. It degrades to O(n)—slower than a simple linear scan!
