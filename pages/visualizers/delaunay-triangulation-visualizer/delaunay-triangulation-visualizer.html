The Empty Circumcircle Rule

A triangulation is strictly "Delaunay" if and only if the circumcircle of EVERY triangle contains absolutely no other points from the set. This simple geometric rule guarantees the triangulation avoids long, thin slivers, making it the mathematical gold standard for physics simulations and 3D modeling.

🔄

Randomized Incremental Construction

Finding the Delaunay Triangulation seems hard, but the incremental algorithm is brilliantly simple. Insert points one by one. Find the triangle it lands in, split it into 3 new triangles, then recursively check the outer edges of the new triangles against the empty-circumcircle rule. If an edge violates it, perform an "Edge Flip". Expected runtime: O(n log n).

🧬

Voronoi Duality

The Voronoi Diagram and the Delaunay Triangulation are mathematical soulmates—exact geometric duals of each other. If you draw a dot at the circumcenter of every Delaunay triangle, and connect the circumcenters of adjacent triangles, you perfectly generate the Voronoi Diagram (regions defining the closest space to a point).
